Agent enablement · AI agents, APIs and MCP

Make your product useful to agents: yours and your customers'.

Add agents that complete real work inside your product, and give assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude safe access to your product's capabilities.

In short: add useful agents to your existing product, and let external agents such as ChatGPT and Claude use your product's capabilities through APIs and MCP, with clear permissions and approvals.

Who it helps

  • Product and engineering leaders at SaaS, marketplace and e-commerce companies with an established product and API.
  • Teams asked for an “AI strategy” that needs to become one shippable workflow.
  • Platforms whose customers already ask ChatGPT or Claude to do work the product could do for them.

Two ways to agent-enable a product

Inside your product

Agents that do the work with your users

Conversational or voice experiences that search, compare, explain, analyse or act inside your product, with the user in control. Example: a live analyst that answers sales questions on screen.

Outside your product

Your product as tools for other agents

Expose chosen capabilities through APIs and an MCP server so assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude can use them on a customer's behalf, with permissions and approvals you define.

Problems it addresses

  • An AI feature that demos well but does not change any customer outcome.
  • Customers rebuilding your workflows in ChatGPT or Claude by copy-pasting data out of your product.
  • No agreement on which actions an agent may take alone, which need approval, and how mistakes are recovered.
  • APIs designed for integrations, not for agents that need small, well-described, safe tools.

What you get

  1. Agent-readiness review. Your workflows, data, APIs, auth and permissions, assessed for what an agent could safely do today.
  2. Opportunity map and first workflow. Ranked candidates, and one workflow chosen for a clear customer or business outcome.
  3. Experience design. The interaction, the level of autonomy at each step, approvals, failure handling and hand-backs to your UI.
  4. Tool contract. The tools an agent needs (inputs, outputs, errors, permissions), as a spec for your API, an MCP server or a ChatGPT app.
  5. Working prototype. An embedded agent or an MCP-based integration running against your sandbox or sample data.
  6. Evaluation plan. Test cases, success measures and what to log, so you can tell whether it works before and after launch.
  7. Build plan. What your team needs to take it to production, in what order.

How an engagement works

  1. Intro call. Your product, customers and the workflow you have in mind.
  2. Readiness review. APIs, data and permissions, plus a short list of candidate workflows.
  3. Design sprint. Experience, autonomy levels and tool contract for the chosen workflow.
  4. Prototype. A working version your team and customers can try.
  5. Evaluate and hand over. Results against the agreed measures, and a build plan for production.

Scope, timing and fees are agreed after the intro call.

Questions

Do we need an MCP server, an API or a ChatGPT app?

It depends on where your customers want to work. A clean API is the foundation. An MCP server exposes chosen capabilities as tools that agents such as ChatGPT and Claude can call. A ChatGPT app, built with OpenAI's Apps SDK on top of MCP, adds an interface inside ChatGPT. The guide explains the trade-offs; the first phase of an engagement decides which you need.

Will agents get access to data users should not see?

They should not. The design starts from your existing permission model: an agent acts on behalf of a signed-in user and can only reach what that user could reach. Actions that change data or spend money get explicit approval steps.

Which models do you work with?

The demos on this site use Google's Gemini Live models for voice and tool calling. Engagements are model-agnostic; the choice depends on your latency, cost, privacy and hosting needs.

Can you work with our engineering team?

Yes. The usual pattern is to design the experience and tool contract together, build a working prototype, then hand over a build plan your engineers own. Aditya can also build alongside them.

Won't this send customers away from our product?

It can, if you expose everything. The work identifies which workflows are better completed inside a conversation and which should bring people back into your product, and designs the hand-off between them.

Have you shipped MCP servers or ChatGPT apps for clients?

No client MCP or ChatGPT-app work is published here. What is published are working demos with code that use the same tool-calling pattern, and research on how agents could use specific platforms' APIs.
