Agent-native agreements

Four principles that make a document platform agent-native, each shown as one end-to-end flow with the metric to watch.

By Aditya Vithaldas

What this is: independent research from Frontier Labs, not client work. Adapted from a narrated, voice-guided concept. Independent proposal, not affiliated with or endorsed by PandaDoc.

A proposal · four principles

What it takes to make PandaDoc agent-native

Here’s a proposal for making PandaDoc agent-native. Contracts are a natural fit for agents: many teams, repeat work, and a clear finish line, a signature. Four principles. Access. A visual write surface. Intelligence. And proactivity. For each one: a simple case, how it could grow, and what already exists today.

Why contracts suit agents: 1 Many teams touch every contract 2 The same work repeats 3 A clear finish line: the signature

  • Access Available wherever your customers already work. Grows into: every surface, every system, every role
  • Write surface Not just text: visual, right inside the agent. Grows into: the right view for each person
  • Intelligence Brings legal’s judgement, not just warnings. Grows into: knowing what legal cares about
  • Proactivity Acts before anyone has to ask. Grows into: acting, not just alerting

01 · Access · proposed flow

Use PandaDoc from the agent you already use

Access. I ask my own agent for an NDA. Through PandaDoc’s MCP server, it drafts it, gets legal’s approval, and sends it for signature. I never open PandaDoc. The metric: requests completed end to end, inside the agent.

Through PandaDoc’s MCP server, the agreement opens inside the conversation.

Metric: Requests completed end to end inside the agent

Outcome: Request to signature in one conversation

01 · Access · today and next

From a live preview to real depth

PandaDoc already has the foundation: a hosted MCP server, and apps in ChatGPT and Claude with a live document preview. What I’d add is depth. Approvals routed through the agent. The deal pulled in from the CRM, and the signed copy filed back. Roles, so sales can draft but only legal can change liability. And more surfaces: Slack, voice, and EU accounts, where the Claude connector isn’t available yet. The tech gap: approval steps and role permissions exposed as agent tools.

Technology layer

Live today

  • 32 MCP tools at mcp.pandadoc.com (EU: .eu)
  • ChatGPT app, Claude connector, and a plugin with 10 skills for Claude Code, Cursor and Gemini CLI

Gap to build

  • Approve and reject tools for approvers; group.name added to selected_approvers
  • Clause and field locks by role, checked on documents_update
  • Works in ChatGPT and Claude (Live today) Today: Create, edit, send, remind and track with 32 tools; 10 workflow skills in the plugin
  • Approvals: routed today, approved in chat next (Partly live) Today: documents_send routes a draft into its chain; templates_approval_workflow_update sets one up Change: Approve and reject tools for the approver; fix selected_approvers, which fails on the missing group.name
  • Deal in from the CRM, signed copy back (Partly live) Today: documents_link attaches a document to a HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive or Dynamics record Change: Let documents_create take that record and fill Client.* tokens and pricing from it
  • Roles: who may change what (Recommended) Today: Access is chosen per user at sign-in, not per clause or field Change: Locks by role on documents_update: sales edits dates and parties, only Legal edits liability
  • More surfaces, and EU accounts (Recommended) Today: EU accounts add mcp.pandadoc.eu by hand in ChatGPT; the Claude connector is global only Change: One listing that routes to the right region at sign-in; the same server behind Slack and voice

02 · Write surface · proposed flow

Ask in words. See it on the page.

Write surface. I ask in text. The document renders right beside the chat, fields filled, and the one change highlighted. Legal sees deviations. Sales sees status. The metric: time to review a draft.

Every change lands in the live document and glows, so you can check it.

Metric: Time to review a draft

Outcome: Reviewed and approved without leaving the conversation

02 · Write surface · today and next

From one preview to the right view for everyone

Today, the ChatGPT app shows the document inline and opens a full-screen editor. The next step is making that view work for each person. Explain what changed since the last version, in plain words. Show legal the deviations from the playbook. Show sales the blockers and the close date, and finance the payment terms. And give the other side a summary they can respond to. The tech: views that know your role, a plain-language version diff, and memory of what each person looks at.

Technology layer

Live today

  • ChatGPT widget: block editing, variable popovers, full-screen view
  • documents_summary_get: headline, short or detailed

Gap to build

  • Widget views by role, built from fields documents_details_get already returns
  • documents_update returns before → after for each change
  • Accept or reject on AI edits, with the view each person last used
  • Inline preview and full-screen editor (Live today) Today: ChatGPT: edit blocks and fill variables in a modal. Claude: inline preview
  • Explains what changed (Recommended) Today: documents_update returns only a confirmation; the web assistant’s edits land with no highlight Change: Return a change list (block, before, after), highlighted in the widget with accept or reject
  • Legal sees deviations (Recommended) Today: Nothing compares a draft with the template it came from Change: A template diff tool: which clauses moved from the approved template, shown as a badge
  • Sales and finance see their part (Recommended) Today: documents_details_get gives everyone one view; grand_total has read 0 on a priced deal Change: Role views over existing fields: status and expiration_date for sales, pricing.total for finance
  • The other side responds (Recommended) Today: Recipients get an email link; summaries serve only the sender Change: Put the headline summary in the send and reminder emails, with replies routed back to the chat

03 · Intelligence · proposed flow

Decisions, not warnings

Intelligence. Instead of warning that liability is uncapped, the agent recommends a cap from your playbook and applies it in one click. When I make an exception, it remembers the reason, the scope and the expiry. The metric: fewer redlines per contract.

The agent brings the playbook. You approve in one click, and it remembers why.

Metric: Redlines per contract

Outcome: Fewer legal round-trips

03 · Intelligence · today and next

From answers to judgement

PandaDoc’s AI can already search, summarize, and answer questions across documents. The recommendation is to go from answers to judgement. Learn which clauses legal always edits, and which drafts get signed untouched. Learn each reviewer’s red lines, and where a counterparty pushes back, with a fallback ready. Spot what’s missing, like a data processing agreement when personal data is in scope. The tech: clause-level history, playbooks learned from past deals, and memory with a scope and an expiry.

Technology layer

Live today

  • documents_search, three summary levels, AI Assistant (beta)
  • 100+ AI-extracted fields on signed contracts

Gap to build

  • Clause history from template to signature
  • Extraction on drafts, and filters on extracted values
  • Exceptions stored with a reason, a scope and an expiry
  • Search, summarize, answer (Live today) Today: Full-text search, summaries, and extracted terms on completed contracts
  • Learns what legal edits (Recommended) Today: Extraction runs after signing; edits between template and signature aren’t kept as data Change: Store clause-level diffs per contract; report the most-edited clauses per template
  • Knows each reviewer (Recommended) Today: approval_execution already records who approved or rejected, with their message Change: Learn from those messages; pre-check a draft against each approver’s past rejections
  • Knows the counterparty (Recommended) Today: Metadata can’t be filtered: finding every Beta contract means full-text search, then reading each Change: A filter on extracted fields such as Counterparty name, plus what each one redlined
  • Spots what’s missing (Recommended) Today: Term checkboxes (audit rights, exclusivity…) appear only after signing Change: Run extraction on the draft and flag missing terms before sending

04 · Proactivity · today and next

From alerts to action

The new contract repository already tracks renewal dates, sends alerts, and builds a daily digest of renewals and stalled deals. The gap is acting on them. Draft the renewal, not just the alert. Nudge a proposal that’s opened but never signed. Draft the order form when a deal is won. And track every obligation, not just renewals. The tech: triggers from the CRM and signatures, obligations pulled from every contract, and clear levels of approval for what the agent may do alone.

Technology layer

Live today

  • Renewal fields: action deadline, term, renewal type, opt-out
  • Renewal alerts, AI Digest, documents_reminder_send

Gap to build

  • PandaDoc webhooks and CRM stage changes passed to agents as triggers
  • Payment, notice and delivery dates extracted, each with an owner
  • Rules for acting alone: remind yes, send no
  • Renewal alerts and a daily digest (Live today) Today: Renewal terms extracted from signed contracts; alerts and a daily digest in the app
  • Draft the renewal, not just the alert (Recommended) Today: The alert reaches a person; the plugin’s renewal-radar skill lists renewals when asked Change: At the renewal action deadline minus 30 days, draft from the original template with the old terms filled
  • Nudge stalled proposals (Recommended) Today: documents_reminder_send works when asked; the follow-up skill drafts nudges on request Change: Trigger it from documents_audit_trail_get: viewed 3+ times, unsigned after 5 days
  • Paperwork when a deal is won (Recommended) Today: documents_link ties a document to a deal; nothing fires on a stage change Change: On “Closed won”, draft the order form from the deal; signed quotes go to invoice-handoff
  • Every obligation, not just renewals (Recommended) Today: Extraction covers renewals and term checkboxes, not payment or notice dates Change: Extract dated obligations and assign each to an owner: invoice due → Finance
